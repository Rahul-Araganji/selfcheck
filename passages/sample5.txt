The Revolt of 1857 was a major uprising against the rule of the British East India Company. It began on 10 May 1857 in Meerut, when Indian soldiers, known as sepoys, rebelled against their officers. One immediate cause was the introduction of new cartridges for the Enfield rifle, which were believed to be greased with animal fat. However, there were deeper causes as well. Many farmers were burdened by heavy land taxes, and several Indian rulers had lost their kingdoms under the Doctrine of Lapse. The rebels marched to Delhi and declared Bahadur Shah Zafar, the last Mughal emperor, as their leader. The revolt soon spread to Kanpur, Lucknow, Jhansi and other places. Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi became one of its most famous leaders. Although the British suppressed the revolt by 1858, it had lasting effects. The East India Company's rule ended, and the British Crown took direct control of India through the Government of India Act 1858.
