The water cycle describes how water moves continuously between the Earth's surface and the atmosphere. Heat from the Sun causes water in oceans, rivers and lakes to change into water vapour, a process called evaporation. Plants also release water vapour through their leaves, which is known as transpiration. As the warm, moist air rises, it cools, and the water vapour turns back into tiny droplets. This change is called condensation, and the droplets gather to form clouds. When the droplets join together and become too heavy, they fall back to the ground as rain, snow or hail. This is called precipitation. Some of the water flows over the land into rivers and finally reaches the sea, while some soaks into the ground and becomes groundwater. In India, about 75 percent of the yearly rainfall comes during the southwest monsoon, between June and September. The water cycle is important because it supplies fresh water to people, animals and crops.
